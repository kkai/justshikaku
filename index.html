Just Shikaku

The Shikaku that teaches.

Shikaku hands you a grid of numbers, and you carve it into rectangles until every number sits in a rectangle of exactly that many cells. The rules take a minute. Playing well takes arguments, ones with names like Prime Strips, Lone Reacher, and No Orphans, and those arguments are what this app is about.

Try it

Tap a cell, then tap the opposite corner of the rectangle you want to lay. Tap a laid rectangle to lift it again. This is the board the app's rules lesson uses.

Three numbers, three rectangles, one room.

What no other Shikaku has

A curriculum

Seven named techniques, each with its own lesson on a real position where that technique is the next move. The path runs from the first easy deduction to the counting endgame, and seven seals fill as you learn.

Hints that argue

Ask for help and the reasoning climbs a ladder: a nudge, then the technique's name, then the full argument drawn on your board. You see the candidates that could work, the ones that die under hatching, and the cell that decides it, with the technique lettered above the grid. The app can lay the rectangle for you, but that is the ladder's last rung and it applies exactly one argued move. Your solve stays yours.

A daily that teaches

Everyone gets the same room each day, and each day's room is built around one named technique. The week climbs the curriculum, a full week earns its own seal, and missed days stay playable because the boards are deterministic.

The proof

After every win, the app replays your own solve and checks each rectangle against its solver. Moves it can derive from the position you played them in count as worked out; moves a hint laid are marked as shown; correct moves beyond the curriculum's reach are called what they are, a leap. No other puzzle app can tell you which of your own moves were reasoned, because no other engine in this category knows why a move is right.

Training that is training

Spot It serves ten real mid-game positions where one technique is the move; lay the right mat or take a strike, and three strikes end the run. The Climb is room after room up the difficulty ladder under the same three strikes. There is no clock anywhere in either. Accuracy is the game.

Proof behind every board

Before a puzzle reaches you, the engine has verified two things: the board has exactly one solution, and the taught techniques are enough to reach it. Guessing is never required, on any board, at any difficulty. If you are stuck, there is always a next deduction waiting, and the hint ladder will walk you to it.

Privacy

The app collects nothing. No account, no analytics, no advertising. Your progress is stored on your device and never sent anywhere, and apart from the App Store purchase check the app makes no network requests at all. Full privacy policy.

Support

I bought the unlock and it is gone

Settings, then Restore purchases. It is always available, including when the app already thinks it is unlocked. If nothing comes back, check you are signed in with the Apple Account that made the purchase.

Does it work offline?

Yes, entirely. Every board is built on your device. The one network request the app ever makes is asking the App Store whether the unlock has been purchased.

Where does my progress live?

On the device, in the app's own storage. There is no cloud sync, so deleting the app deletes your stats, best times, streak and lesson progress.

This board looks unsolvable

It isn't. Every shipped puzzle is checked twice before you see it: once to prove it has exactly one solution, and once to prove the app's own techniques can finish it. If you are stuck, the hint button will point you at the next deduction. If you still think a board is wrong, mail me and say which one.
