What no other Shikaku has

Shikaku hands you a grid of numbers, and you carve it into rectangles until every number sits in a rectangle of exactly that many cells. The rules take a minute. Playing well takes arguments, ones with names, and no other app in this category names them, teaches them, or checks whether you used them.

A curriculum

Seven named techniques, each with its own lesson on a real position where that technique is the next move. The path runs from the first easy deduction to the counting endgame, and seven seals fill as you learn.

Hints that argue

Ask for help and the reasoning climbs a ladder: a nudge, then the technique's name, then the full argument drawn on your board, exactly like the demo above. The app can lay the rectangle for you, but that is the ladder's last rung and it applies one argued move. Your solve stays yours.

A daily that teaches

Everyone gets the same room each day, and each day's room is built around one named technique. The week climbs the curriculum, a full week earns its own seal, and missed days stay playable because the boards are deterministic.

The proof

After every win the app replays your own solve and checks each rectangle against its solver. Moves it can derive from the position you played them in count as worked out, moves a hint laid are marked as shown, and correct moves beyond the curriculum's reach are called what they are, a leap. No other puzzle app can tell you which of your own moves were reasoned, because no other engine here knows why a move is right.

Training that is training

Spot It serves ten real mid-game positions where one technique is the move. The Climb is room after room up the difficulty ladder. Both end on three strikes, and neither has a clock, because accuracy is the game.

Privacy

The app collects nothing. No account, no analytics, no advertising. Your progress is stored on your device and never sent anywhere, and apart from the App Store purchase check the app makes no network requests at all. Full privacy policy.

Support

I bought the unlock and it is gone

Settings, then Restore purchases. It is always available, including when the app already thinks it is unlocked. If nothing comes back, check you are signed in with the Apple Account that made the purchase.

Does it work offline?

Yes, entirely. Every board is built on your device. The one network request the app ever makes is asking the App Store whether the unlock has been purchased.

Where does my progress live?

On the device, in the app's own storage. There is no cloud sync, so deleting the app deletes your stats, best times, streak and lesson progress.

This board looks unsolvable

It isn't. Every shipped puzzle is checked twice before you see it: once to prove it has exactly one solution, and once to prove the app's own techniques can finish it.
