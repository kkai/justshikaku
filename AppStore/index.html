Shikaku

Learn to lay the mats.

Shikaku is a puzzle of rectangles. A grid holds a few numbers, and your job is to split the whole grid into rectangles so that each one contains exactly one number, and that number is the rectangle's area. A 6 sits in a rectangle of six cells, a 1 in a single cell. That is the whole rulebook. Getting good at it is another matter, and that is what the app is for.

Try it

Tap a cell, then tap the opposite corner of the rectangle you want to lay. Tap a laid rectangle to lift it again. This is the board the app's rules lesson uses.

Three numbers, three rectangles, one room.

How it teaches

Most puzzle apps leave you to work out the technique yourself, or hand you the answer when you are stuck. Just Shikaku does neither.

Seven techniques, one idea at a time

The game runs on seven named techniques: Ones, Prime Strips, Only Fit, Common Ground, Lone Reacher, No Orphans and Count the Room. Each has its own lesson, a short board where the technique is the way through rather than a page of text to read, and the sequence runs from the first easy deduction to the counting endgame.

Hints that name the technique

Ask for a hint and the app points you at a region first. Ask again and it names the technique and states the rule. Ask again and it draws the argument on the board. Only the last step lays the rectangle for you, and taking it is a deliberate act.

Mastery you actually earned

Practice drills rehearse one technique at a time. Progress counts only the deductions you worked out unaided, so the path reflects what you can really do.

No guessing, ever

Every puzzle has exactly one solution, and every puzzle can be finished by reasoning alone. The app proves both before a board ever reaches you. If you are stuck, it is never because the board is unfair; there is always a next deduction waiting to be found.

Privacy

The app collects nothing. No account, no analytics, no advertising. Your progress is stored on your device and never sent anywhere, and apart from the App Store purchase check the app makes no network requests at all. Full privacy policy.

Support

I bought the unlock and it is gone

Settings, then Restore purchases. It is always available, including when the app already thinks it is unlocked. If nothing comes back, check you are signed in with the Apple Account that made the purchase.

Does it work offline?

Yes, entirely. Puzzles are generated on your device. The only time the app talks to the network is to ask the App Store whether the unlock has been purchased.

Where is my progress kept?

On the device, in the app's own storage. There is no cloud sync, so deleting the app deletes your stats, best times and lesson progress.

This board looks unsolvable

It isn't. Every shipped puzzle is checked twice before you see it: once to prove it has exactly one solution, and once to prove the app's own techniques can finish it. If you are stuck, the hint button will point you at the next deduction. If you still think a board is wrong, mail me and say which one.
